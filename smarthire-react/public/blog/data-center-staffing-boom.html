Data Center Staffing Boom: Which IT Roles US Companies Are Hiring For

By Omkesh Singh Thakur · October 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Ask most people which tech jobs are hot in 2026 and they'll point to generative AI engineers. Ask anyone trying to staff a hyperscale data center and you'll hear something completely different: electricians, liquid cooling engineers, and critical facility technicians. The buildout behind AI and cloud is so massive that hiring, not money, has become the primary bottleneck.

The staffing industry is feeling it too. In Staffing Industry Analysts' (SIA) latest pulse survey, 81% of IT staffing executives said data centers will create moderate or higher demand for technical staffing. Below is a plain-English look at where that demand lands, what the roles pay, and how to position yourself, whether you're a candidate or the person trying to hire one.

Quick takeaways
  • The hardest roles to fill are physical and mission-critical: electricians, critical facility technicians, liquid cooling and cabling specialists.
  • Employers are dropping rigid degree requirements and training people in-house.
  • Competition is fierce: about 25% of data center personnel are hired away by competitors every year.

Why data center hiring is spiking right now

North America already has more than 66 GW of data center capacity, according to JLL's midyear report as cited by Fierce Network, and the build-out is estimated near $700 billion. Every one of those buildings needs certified specialists to wire, cool, commission, and then operate it around the clock.

The workforce hasn't kept up. Industry panels report the skilled-trades gap is stretching project backlogs to nearly a year, and US electrician openings alone stand at roughly 81,000. Around 30% of master electricians are nearing retirement age. A Digital Realty learning leader summed up the pipeline problem: "We don't have data center engineers coming out the door."

Electricians come first

An AI data center is fundamentally a giant power station. Without high-voltage power distribution, nothing else turns on. Per a Per Scholas industry panel, around 30% of electricians are nearing retirement, deepening the crunch every year.

Cooling is the fastest-growing headache

Randstad's analysis of over 50 million postings found demand for HVAC and cooling engineers up 67% since late 2022, and robotics technicians up 107%. Liquid cooling is the reason: modern AI chips produce extreme heat requiring direct-to-chip liquid cold plates and glycol closed loops that older air-cooled facilities never required.

Don't forget the IT side

Not everything requires a hard hat. Cloud, data, cybersecurity, and fiber integration work are core drivers of IT staffing demand. If you're a network, infrastructure, or security engineer, data center operators and their prime contractors are an expanding client base.

Pay and where the jobs are

Data center technician pay runs from $45,000 to $145,000 a year, depending on experience, location, and certifications. Experienced electricians leading multi-site crews in Northern Virginia clear $200,000. Specialists with liquid cooling or advanced fiber skills can reach $250,000 to $300,000. Kelly Services data shows a 25–30% pay bump for people who move from standard commercial trades into data center environments.

Geographically, Virginia, Texas, Ohio, and Iowa come up most often as hiring hotspots. Poaching is routine: Kelly's survey found that roughly 25% of data center personnel get hired away by competitors every year.

If you want in: what to do

You may need less of a "tech" background than you think. Digital Realty and other colocation operators are now recruiting licensed electricians, HVAC techs, and career changers with mechanical aptitude, then training them in-house:

  • Highlight transferable trade experience: power, HVAC, industrial controls, structured cabling, and safety.
  • Add recognized credentials such as OSHA 10/30, NFPA 70E awareness, or a data center credential (CDCP and BICSI are common names). Check our guide to the 10 highest-paying IT certifications in 2026.
  • Be open to travel and project work; many builds are commissioned on contracts. Review C2C vs W2 vs 1099 for IT contractors to maximize your tax deductions.

If you're hiring: what works

When a quarter of your technicians can be lured away, process speed matters as much as pay. Skills-based screening, short interview loops, and contract-to-hire options consistently beat waiting for a perfect resume. Frequently asked questions

Which data center jobs are in highest demand in the US?

Electricians, critical facility technicians, HVAC and liquid cooling engineers, low-voltage and fiber specialists, and commissioning engineers are the most constrained roles in 2026.

Do you need a degree for data center jobs?

Often no. Operators like Digital Realty hire electricians, HVAC techs, and career changers, then train them on-site for data center engineer roles.

How much do data center technicians earn?

Reported ranges run from about $45,000 to $145,000 a year, with senior leads clearing $200,000+.

Where are most data center jobs located?

Virginia, Texas, Ohio, and Iowa are frequently named as top hiring locations for data center construction and operations.
